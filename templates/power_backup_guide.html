Calculate and plan backup power for your medical equipment

Jump to Calculator Home ICU Setup Guide

India-Specific Guide: This guide is based on the Indian power grid (230V AC / 50Hz). All UPS ratings, battery brands (Exide, Amaron), and kVA specifications follow Indian market standards (BIS). Power outages in India can last 4–12+ hours depending on region, making adequate backup critical for home ICU setups.

Understanding Power Backup

Your Power backup system : Batteries store energy (Amp-hours), devices consume energy (Watts), and the UPS converts and delivers stored energy.

Backup Time = (Battery Capacity in Wh × 0.75) ÷ Total Load in Watts

75% efficiency accounts for battery and UPS inverter conversion losses

Heat Impact (Indian Summers): The 75% efficiency factor assumes moderate temperatures (~25°C). In Indian summers (35–45°C), lead-acid battery capacity can drop an additional 10–20%. If batteries are in a non-AC room during peak summer, expect 60–65% usable capacity instead of 75%. Keep batteries in a cool, ventilated space for optimal performance.

What does 25% usage mean?
If you need 10 hours of backup, 25% usage means the device runs for about 2.5 hours out of those 10 hours. For example, Oxygen concentrator running 2.5 hours during a 10-hour power outage = 2.5/10 = 25% duty cycle.

Option A: Split Load Across Two UPS Units

By dedicating one UPS to the ventilator only, you ensure it runs longest:

UPS 1 — Ventilator Only
Load: 120W
Backup: ~19 hours
2,268 Wh ÷ 120W = 18.9 hrs
UPS 2 — Oxygen + Suction
Load: 172W effective (685W at 25% duty cycle)
Backup: ~13 hours
2,268 Wh ÷ 172W = 13.2 hrs

Advantage: Even if UPS 2 runs out after 13 hours, UPS 1 keeps the ventilator running for 19+ hours.

Option B: One UPS at a Time (Sequential Usage)

Power all devices from one UPS, then switch to second UPS when first is empty:

Combined Battery Capacity: 2 × 2,268 Wh = 4,536 Wh

How it works: Run all devices on UPS 1 → when UPS 1 battery low, switch all plugs to UPS 2 → continues from where UPS 1 stopped.
